What a stack test measures

A stack test, also called a source test or performance test, measures the pollutants leaving a stationary source such as a boiler, kiln, turbine, or incinerator. A crew climbs to sampling ports on the stack, pulls exhaust gas through carefully controlled sampling trains, and follows the EPA reference methods to the letter so the numbers hold up in front of a regulator.

Almost every answer a test produces is a concentration multiplied by a flow. Methods 1 through 4 work out how much gas leaves the stack. Method 5 and the instrumental analyzers work out how much pollutant is in each cubic foot. Multiply them and you have an emission rate you can compare to a permit limit.

Why facilities test: to show compliance with permit limits, New Source Performance Standards (40 CFR 60) and NESHAP rules (40 CFR 63); to measure control device efficiency; and to certify continuous emission monitors.
