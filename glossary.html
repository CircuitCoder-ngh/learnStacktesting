Glossary

86 terms and 29 symbols used across the course

Stack testing has its own vocabulary, and most of it is shorthand. Each entry here says what a term means in plain words and links to the lesson that teaches it.

0–9

8-and-2 rule

The Method 1 siting guideline: put the sampling ports at least 8 stack diameters downstream and 2 diameters upstream of any flow disturbance. A site that meets it can use the minimum number of traverse points.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

A

Acetone blank

A sample of the acetone used to rinse the train, evaporated and weighed like the real rinses. It shows how much residue the solvent itself leaves, which is subtracted from the rinse result within the limit the method allows.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Actual cubic feet per minute (acfm)

Gas flow at the stack’s own temperature, pressure, and moisture, before any correction to standard conditions. Fans and ducts are sized in acfm; emission rates use dry standard flow. Compare Qsd.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

B

Back half

Everything in a sampling train downstream of the filter: impingers and the glassware between them. In Method 5 the back half catch is used for moisture, not filterable particulate; other methods analyze it for condensables, acid gases, or metals. Compare front half.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Bias-corrected concentration

An analyzer’s run-average reading adjusted using the zero and upscale system bias checks made before and after the run, so that small calibration shifts do not carry into the result.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Breeching

The duct that carries exhaust from a boiler or process into the stack. Where it joins the stack is usually the upstream flow disturbance for Method 1.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

B_{ws}Moisture content

The fraction of the stack gas, by volume, that is water vapor. A Bws of 0.12 means 12% water. Measured by Method 4.

Taught in Method 4: moisture

C

Calibration error

For an instrumental analyzer, the difference between its response to a calibration gas injected directly into it and the gas’s certified value, expressed as a percent of the calibration span. Method 7E allows up to 2.0% of span.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Calibration span

The upper end of an analyzer’s calibration range, set by the concentration of the high-level calibration gas. Calibration error, bias, and drift limits are all percentages of it.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

CEMS

Continuous emission monitoring system: analyzers and often a flow monitor permanently installed on a stack, reporting emissions around the clock. CEMS are certified and audited against reference method tests in a RATA.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Chain of custody

The signed record that follows each sample from collection to analysis, showing who held it and when. It is the evidence that the sample analyzed is the sample collected, unaltered.

Taught in The test, start to finish

Chemiluminescence

The light given off when NO reacts with ozone. Chemiluminescent analyzers measure NO this way, and measure NOx by first converting NO2 to NO. Method 7E is built around them.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Condensable particulate matter (CPM)

Material that is a vapor at stack temperature but condenses into particles once the plume cools after leaving the stack. Method 202 measures it in the impingers downstream of a filter.

Taught in More methods

C_pPitot tube coefficient

The correction factor that relates the pressure difference a pitot tube reads to the true velocity pressure. A Type S pitot tube that meets the geometric specifications can be assigned 0.84; a standard pitot tube is about 0.99.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

Cyclonic flow

Swirling flow in which gas spirals up the stack instead of moving straight up. Method 1 checks for it with the null-angle technique; if the average absolute yaw angle exceeds 20°, the site is unacceptable without corrective action or an alternative method.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

D

\Delta HOrifice pressure differential

The pressure drop across the calibrated orifice in the meter box, in inches of water. It shows, and lets the operator set, the sampling rate. To stay isokinetic, ΔH is reset at every traverse point from the K-factor.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

\Delta H_@Orifice coefficient

The orifice pressure differential that gives 0.75 cfm of air at 68 °F and 29.92 in. Hg. It is determined when the meter box is calibrated and is used to work out the K-factor.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

\Delta pVelocity head

The pressure difference between the two openings of a pitot tube, in inches of water. Velocity is proportional to its square root, so the average velocity uses the average of √Δp, not the square root of the average Δp.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

Disturbance

Anything that changes the flow pattern in a duct: bends, expansions, contractions, fans, dampers, control devices, the stack exit, or a visible flame. Method 1 distances are measured from them.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

Drift

The change in an analyzer system’s response to the same calibration gas between the start and end of a run. Method 7E allows up to 3.0% of the calibration span.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Dry gas meter

The positive-displacement meter in the meter box that totals the volume of gas sampled, after the water has been removed by the impingers. Its readings are corrected with the meter’s Y factor.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Dry standard cubic foot (dscf)

One cubic foot of gas with its water vapor removed, at standard conditions of 68 °F and 29.92 in. Hg. Concentrations and flows in EPA Part 60 methods are reported on this basis.

Taught in Worked example

Dry standard cubic meter (dscm)

The metric equivalent of a dscf: one cubic meter of dry gas at 20 °C and 760 mm Hg. One dscm is 35.31 dscf.

Taught in Worked example

E

Emission rate E

The mass of pollutant released per unit of time, such as lb/hr, or per unit of heat input or production, such as lb/MMBtu. Usually concentration times flow.

Taught in Worked example

EPA Protocol gas

A calibration gas certified under EPA’s traceability protocol, so its concentration is traceable to national reference standards. Instrumental methods require Protocol gases for calibration.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Equal-area method

The Method 1 approach of dividing the stack cross-section into equal areas and placing one traverse point in each, so that a simple average of the readings equals the area-weighted average.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

Equivalent diameter D_e

For a rectangular duct, 2LW ÷ (L + W). Method 1 uses it in place of a diameter when measuring distances from disturbances.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

Excess air

Combustion air supplied beyond what is needed to burn the fuel completely, as a percent. Calculated from the O2, CO, and N2 measured by Method 3.

Taught in Method 3: gas composition

F

F-factor F_d

The volume of dry combustion gas produced per unit of heat in a fuel, in dscf/MMBtu, from Method 19. It converts a concentration to lb/MMBtu. Typical Fd values: natural gas 8,710; bituminous coal 9,780; oil 9,190.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Filter box

The heated enclosure that holds the filter at the method’s temperature; in Method 5, 248 ± 25 °F (120 ± 14 °C). Also called the hot box or oven.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Filterable particulate matter (FPM)

Particulate that is solid or liquid at the filter temperature and is caught on the filter or in the nozzle, probe, and filter holder ahead of it. This is what Method 5 measures.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Flame ionization detector (FID)

An analyzer that burns the sample in a hydrogen flame and measures the ions produced, which is proportional to the organic carbon present. Method 25A uses an FID for total hydrocarbons, usually calibrated with propane.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Front half

The part of a sampling train up to and including the filter: nozzle, probe liner, filter holder front half, and filter. The Method 5 particulate result is the front half catch.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

FTIR

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy, which identifies and measures many gases at once from their infrared absorption. Method 320 covers extractive FTIR.

Taught in More methods

Fyrite

A hand-held absorbing analyzer that measures a single gas, CO2 or O2, by the change in liquid level after the gas is absorbed. Method 3 allows it for molecular weight determinations.

Taught in Method 3: gas composition

G

Grains per dry standard cubic foot (gr/dscf)

A common unit for particulate concentration. There are 7,000 grains in a pound, and one grain is 64.8 mg.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

I

Impinger

A glass vessel through which sample gas is bubbled. In a chilled train, impingers condense the water vapor; with reagents in them, they capture specific pollutants.

Taught in Method 4: moisture

Inclined manometer

A liquid-filled gauge with a sloped tube, so a small pressure difference moves the liquid a long way along the scale. Used to read pitot Δp and orifice ΔH precisely.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

Percent isokinetic \%I

The ratio of the actual sampling velocity at the nozzle to the stack gas velocity, averaged over a run, as a percent. 100% is perfect; Method 5 accepts 90 to 110%.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Isokinetic sampling

Sampling so that gas enters the nozzle at the same velocity as the stack gas flowing past it. Particles then enter the nozzle in the same proportion they are carried in the gas. Method 5 requires each run to be between 90% and 110% isokinetic.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

K

K-factor K

The multiplier that tells the operator what orifice ΔH to set for a given pitot Δp to stay isokinetic: ΔH = K × Δp. It depends on the nozzle size, meter calibration, and stack conditions.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

L

lb/MMBtu

Pounds of pollutant per million Btu of fuel heat input. Common for boiler limits; calculated from concentration, O2 or CO2, and an F-factor.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Leak check

A test that the sampling train or pitot lines are not drawing in outside air. Method 5 requires a post-run leak check at or above the highest vacuum reached, with a leak rate no greater than 0.020 cfm or 4% of the average sampling rate, whichever is less.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

M

M_dDry molecular weight

The molecular weight of the stack gas with water excluded, in lb/lb-mole, calculated from the measured O2, CO2, and (by difference) N2 and CO. Dry air is about 28.84.

Taught in Method 3: gas composition

Meter box

The control console of a sampling train: vacuum pump, dry gas meter, calibrated orifice, manometers for Δp and ΔH, temperature readouts, and heater controls. Also called the control module.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Method 1A

The version of Method 1 for small stacks and ducts, from 4 to 12 inches in diameter.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

M_sWet molecular weight

The molecular weight of the stack gas as it is, including water vapor: Md(1 − Bws) + 18.0 Bws. It goes into the velocity equation.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

N

NDIR

Nondispersive infrared: an analyzer that measures a gas by how much infrared light it absorbs at a characteristic wavelength. Used for CO, CO2, and sometimes SO2.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

NESHAP

National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants, in 40 CFR Parts 61 and 63. Each subpart covers a source category and sets limits and testing requirements.

Taught in The test, start to finish

Nozzle

The sharp-edged inlet at the tip of the probe that faces into the gas flow. Its size is chosen before the test so isokinetic sampling is possible, and its inside diameter is measured to the nearest 0.001 in.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

NSPS

New Source Performance Standards, in 40 CFR Part 60. They apply to new, modified, and reconstructed sources in listed categories and require initial performance tests.

Taught in The test, start to finish

Null angle \alpha

The angle a Type S pitot tube must be rotated from its 0° reference position to read zero. It equals the flow’s yaw angle at that point and is used in the Method 1 cyclonic flow check.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

O

Opacity

How much a plume blocks the view of the background, as a percent. Method 9 measures it by a certified observer reading the plume at 15-second intervals.

Taught in More methods

Orsat

A glass absorption analyzer that measures CO2, O2, and CO in turn by passing a measured gas volume through chemical absorbents and recording the volume lost to each.

Taught in Method 3: gas composition

Oxygen correction

Adjusting a concentration to a reference O2 level so results from different amounts of excess air can be compared. For 7% O2: C × (20.9 − 7) ÷ (20.9 − measured %O2).

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

P

Paramagnetic analyzer

An O2 analyzer that exploits oxygen’s strong attraction to a magnetic field. A common choice for Method 3A.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

P_{bar}Barometric pressure

Atmospheric pressure at the sampling location, in inches of mercury. Read on site, or from a nearby station and corrected for the elevation difference to the platform.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

P_gStatic pressure

The pressure of the stack gas relative to the atmosphere, in inches of water. Negative when a fan pulls gas through the duct, positive when it pushes.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

Platform

The work surface at the sampling ports. Under the NSPS general provisions the facility must provide safe platforms and safe access to them.

Taught in The test, start to finish

Port

An opening in the stack wall for sampling, usually a short pipe nipple with a cap or flange. Circular stacks normally have two, 90° apart.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

ppmvd

Parts per million by volume, dry basis: the concentration a gas would have if the water vapor were removed.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Probe liner

The inner tube of the heated sampling probe, usually borosilicate glass or quartz, that carries the sample from the nozzle to the filter.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

P_sAbsolute stack pressure

Barometric pressure plus static pressure, in inches of mercury: Pbar + Pg ÷ 13.6.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

Q

Q_{sd}Dry standard flow rate

The stack gas flow rate with water removed and corrected to standard conditions, in dscf/hr. Emission rates are concentration times Qsd.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

R

RATA

Relative accuracy test audit: a series of reference method runs (at least nine) made side by side with a CEMS to certify or audit it. The relative accuracy must fall within the limit in the applicable performance specification.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Reference method

A test procedure EPA specifies for measuring a pollutant, such as Methods 1 through 5. Using another method generally requires the regulator’s approval.

Taught in The test, start to finish

Representative conditions

The process operating conditions a rule or permit requires during a test, often the highest normal operating rate. Results only apply to the conditions they were measured under.

Taught in The test, start to finish

Response time

How long an analyzer system takes to reach 95% of a stable reading after a step change in calibration gas. Each traverse point is sampled for at least twice this time.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

Run

One complete sampling period. Most tests consist of three runs, and compliance is judged on their average.

Taught in The test, start to finish

S

Sample recovery

Removing the collected sample from the train after a run: recovering the filter, rinsing the front half, measuring impinger contents, and sealing and labeling every container.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Sampling train

The connected assembly that collects the sample: nozzle, probe, filter, impingers, umbilical, and meter box.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Silica gel

A desiccant in the last impinger that absorbs the water the others miss. It is weighed before and after for the moisture result; indicating gel changes color when it is spent.

Taught in Method 4: moisture

Site-specific test plan

The written plan for a test, also called a test protocol: methods, sampling locations, operating conditions, quality assurance, and schedule. Many NESHAP let the agency require one and review it before the test.

Taught in The test, start to finish

Standard conditions

The reference temperature and pressure for EPA Part 60 methods: 68 °F (528 °R) and 29.92 in. Hg, or 20 °C and 760 mm Hg.

Taught in Worked example

Stratification

Uneven gas concentration across a stack cross-section. A stratification test decides whether instrumental sampling can use one point, three points, or a full traverse.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

System bias

The difference between an analyzer’s response to calibration gas sent through the whole sampling system from the probe and its response to the same gas injected directly. Method 7E allows up to 5.0% of span.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers

T

T_mDry gas meter temperature

The average temperature of the gas at the dry gas meter during a run. Used in degrees Rankine (°F + 460) to correct the metered volume to standard conditions.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Traverse point

One of the measurement locations inside the stack cross-section chosen by Method 1. Sampling moves from point to point, spending equal time at each.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

T_sStack temperature

The temperature of the stack gas, measured with a thermocouple at each traverse point. Used in degrees Rankine (°F + 460) in the equations.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

Type S pitot tube

A pitot tube with two opposed openings, one facing into the flow and one facing away. Its large openings resist plugging in dusty gas, making it the standard field instrument for Method 2. The S stands for Stausscheibe, German for “stagnation disk.”

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

U

Umbilical

The bundled line connecting the sampling box on the platform to the meter box: sample hose, pitot lines, thermocouple wires, and heater power.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

V

V_{m(std)}Sample volume at standard conditions

The dry gas volume measured by the meter, corrected for the meter’s Y factor and to standard temperature and pressure, in dscf.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

v_sStack gas velocity

The average speed of the gas through the stack, in ft/s, from the pitot readings, temperature, pressure, and molecular weight.

Taught in Method 2: velocity and flow rate

Y

Y factor Y

The dry gas meter calibration factor: the ratio of the true volume from a reference to the volume the meter reads. A post-test check must agree with the pre-test value within 5%.

Taught in Method 5: particulate matter

Yaw angle

The angle between the local gas flow and the stack axis. See null angle.

Taught in Method 1: traverse points

Z

Zero gas

A calibration gas with essentially none of the measured pollutant, such as nitrogen or purified air, used for the low-level calibration checks.

Taught in Instrumental analyzers
